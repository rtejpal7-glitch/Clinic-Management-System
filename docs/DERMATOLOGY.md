# Dermatology module: clinical decision support and prescription intelligence

Version 0.2 extends ClinicFlow for dermatology clinics. All earlier features are unchanged. This document covers what was added, how each part works, and its limits.

> **Read this first.** ClinicFlow does not diagnose patients or prescribe medicines. The decision-support panels are reading aids for a qualified doctor, who makes every decision. The starter clinical content was **drafted by an AI assistant**. It is marked `STARTER_DRAFT`, it has **not** been verified against its sources, and it is not used until a clinician approves it. Prescribing statistics describe what doctors prescribed. They do not show that a treatment works. ClinicFlow is not a certified medical device, and it is not NABH- or ABDM-certified.

## 1. Workflow

```
Waiting queue → Start consultation
  ├─ Notes, vitals & diagnosis        (unchanged; ICD-10 working diagnosis)
  ├─ Dermatology assessment           (coded findings + background + pregnancy status + photographs)
  ├─ Decision support (A · B · C)     (three panels side by side; "Add" copies a suggestion into the draft)
  ├─ Prescription & orders            (edit everything; source tag shows where an item came from; safety check)
  └─ Finalize → safety acknowledgement if needed → record locked
       └─ Decisions & outcome          (suggested vs prescribed, overrides, follow-up outcome)
```

The consultation screen shows the dermatology tabs when the clinic's specialty is `DERMATOLOGY` (`facilities.specialty_code`). Other specialties can plug in later: a consultation stores `specialty_code`, and specialty logic runs through the `ConsultationExtension` interface (`backend/src/service/ConsultationExtension.hpp`).

## 2. Dermatology assessment

The assessment is mostly coded, so it is quick to fill in and machine-readable. Each section is a set of chips or drop-downs:

- **Symptoms and history:** type of problem, duration, severity, symptoms (itching, pain, burning, fever, bleeding, breathing difficulty, …), spreading, recurring, previous episodes, aggravating and relieving factors.
- **Examination:** body sites, lesion types, colour, configuration, distribution, extent (% body surface area) and free-text findings.
- **Background:** other conditions, family history, previous skin treatments and the response to them, and pregnancy or breastfeeding status. Allergies and current medicines stay on the patient record and the notes.

The vocabulary is defined once in `backend/src/derm/DermVocabulary.hpp` and served at `GET /derm/vocabulary`. Unknown codes are rejected. The assessment locks with the consultation (database trigger).

**Clinical photographs.** A photograph can only be taken after the doctor ticks a consent box, and the consent scope is stored with it. The browser resizes the image. The server accepts only JPEG, PNG or WebP (checked by file content, not file name), up to 6 MB, and stores it encrypted with AES-256-GCM. Images are only served through an authorised endpoint, with `Cache-Control: no-store`. Every view is written to the audit log. Photographs are never deleted; a wrong one is marked "entered in error". Permissions: `photo.read` and `photo.write` (doctors only by default).

## 3. Panel A: Clinical knowledge

**Engine:** `backend/src/derm/CdsEngine.cpp`. It is pure and deterministic, and it is unit-tested.

1. The recorded findings become tokens, such as `LESION:WHEAL`, `SITE:FINGER_WEBS` and `AGE:ADOLESCENT`.
2. **Minimum information.** Panel A needs the problem type, a lesion type or body site, and the duration. If any is missing, the panel shows *Not enough information yet* and lists what is missing. It does not guess.
3. **Matching.** Each approved condition profile has weighted findings. The panel shows the share of the profile's weight that was recorded. Only conditions with at least 25% match are listed, at most five. They are labelled *Strong*, *Moderate* or *Weak match*, never as a probability. Each one shows the findings that matched and the typical findings that were not recorded.
4. **Distinguishing questions** come from the three closest matches.
5. **Red flags** are checked on every run, even when other information is missing. Examples: possible SJS/TEN, angioedema or anaphylaxis, ophthalmic zoster, a changing pigmented lesion, a non-healing ulcer, erythroderma, spreading cellulitis with fever.
6. **Guideline treatment options** are filtered by the recorded severity and linked to catalogue products. Each option shows its citation. It is also checked against this patient (allergy, pregnancy, age).
7. Each run is stored with its input, the engine version and the result (`cds_analysis_runs`). What the doctor saw is snapshotted (`suggestion_snapshots`).

## 4. Panel B: My prescribing patterns

**Source:** `prescribing_cases`. When a dermatology consultation with an assessment is finalized, one coded row is written: primary ICD-10 code, severity, age band, sex, pregnancy status, duration band, sites, lesions, and the medicines with route, frequency and duration. Later outcomes are added to the same row. No statistic is entered by hand or generated.

**Comparable cases:** only the same doctor's finalized cases with the same primary diagnosis, excluding the current consultation. The panel tries the narrowest comparison first and widens it only if needed:

1. same diagnosis, severity and age band;
2. same diagnosis and severity;
3. same diagnosis.

It always states which comparison it used. Pregnant patients are compared only with pregnant cases, and other patients never with pregnant cases.

**Display:** the panel needs at least 3 comparable cases. For each medicine it shows the percentage of cases, the case count, the doctor's usual regimen, and recorded follow-up outcomes (only when at least 3 are recorded). It also shows the data period. Each medicine is checked against the current patient.

## 5. Panel C: ClinicFlow network

**Who takes part.** A clinic joins under a recorded data-sharing agreement reference (*Clinic & network*, `settings.manage`). Only participating clinics contribute, and only their doctors can view network patterns.

**What is shared.** At finalization, if the clinic participates and the patient has not opted out, one record is written to `network_case_records` containing:

- the diagnosis code, severity, age band, sex, pregnancy flag, duration band, medicines and regimen, and outcome;
- the **month** (no exact date);
- **keyed HMAC pseudonyms** for the clinic, the doctor and the source case.

There is no patient ID, name, number, date of birth or free text. The source-case pseudonym lets a later outcome or withdrawal update the record without revealing the case.

**Disclosure controls**, applied in `derm::summarizeNetwork` and unit-tested:

- at least **10 cases**, **3 doctors** and **2 clinics**;
- no clinic may contribute more than **70%** of the cases;
- medicines used in fewer than 3 cases, or by only one doctor, are not listed separately;
- when statistics are withheld, the exact count is hidden too.

The viewing doctor's own cases are excluded. The comparison widens in the same way as Panel B. Data is limited to the last 36 months.

**Withdrawal.**

- If a clinic leaves the network, its records are withdrawn immediately.
- If a patient opts out (patient profile), their contributed records are withdrawn.

**Caveats shown in the UI:** network data is not representative of all dermatologists, and how often a medicine is prescribed is not evidence that it works.

## 6. Prescription safety checks

**Logic:** `backend/src/derm/MedicationSafety.cpp`. It runs on the saved draft (`POST /consultations/{id}/safety-check`) and again at finalization, for every specialty.

| Check | Level |
|---|---|
| Recorded medication allergy matches the medicine (name, ingredient or class) | Contraindicated |
| Pregnancy, breastfeeding or age rule (e.g. tetracyclines under 8) | Contraindicated (approved content) or warning |
| Same ingredient twice (including combination products) | Warning |
| Interaction pair in the catalogue | Contraindicated / major → warning / moderate → info |
| High-alert medicine (MOM.2) | Info |
| WHO AWaRe Watch/Reserve antibiotic, or restricted under clinic policy (COP.5) | Info / warning plus justification |
| Pregnancy status not recorded for a woman of child-bearing age when relevant | Info |

Alerts that come from unreviewed content are capped at *warning* and labelled as draft information.

**Contraindicated** alerts and **restricted antimicrobials** block finalization until the doctor either changes the prescription or records a reason. The response is `409 SAFETY_ACKNOWLEDGEMENT_REQUIRED`. The reason is stored in `safety_overrides` and audited as `SAFETY_OVERRIDE`.

## 7. Decision tracking and outcomes

- Suggestions never enter the prescription by themselves. **Add** copies a product into the draft with `sourceKind` and the snapshot it came from. The server checks that the snapshot belongs to the same consultation.
- At finalization, ClinicFlow compares each panel's latest suggestions with the final prescription and records one of four results per suggestion, with the user and timestamp:
  - `ACCEPTED`
  - `MODIFIED` (dose, frequency or duration changed)
  - `NOT_USED`
  - `DOCTOR_ADDED` (prescribed without any suggestion)
- The *Decisions & outcome* tab shows *suggested vs prescribed*, any overrides, and which panels were viewed.
- **Follow-up outcomes** can be recorded on finalized consultations: `RESOLVED`, `IMPROVED`, `UNCHANGED` or `WORSENED`, with adverse effects. They are append-only and audited, and they feed Panels B and C.

## 8. Clinical content governance

- **Review roles.** Only users with `cds.review` (the `CLINICAL_REVIEWER` role) can approve, retire or reopen content. Approval requires a note saying what the item was checked against. A medicine also needs a reference before it can be approved. Administrators (`catalogue.manage`) can edit medicine information. Any edit sends the item back to *awaiting review*.
- **What decision support uses.** Only `APPROVED` content: conditions, red flags, medicine dosing defaults and contraindication levels, and interactions. The panel says how many items are still waiting for review.
- **Generic and brand.** Generic rows hold the reviewed information. Brands added by a clinic share the generic code.

## 9. Analytics

- **Practice insights → My practice** (`insights.read`): the signed-in doctor's own data only. It covers today's appointments, pending and completed visits, follow-ups due, recent prescriptions, frequent medicines and diagnoses (90 days), monthly trend, recorded outcomes, and how decision support was used.
- **Practice insights → ClinicFlow network** (`network.read`): diagnosis-level counts that meet the thresholds. No patient information appears.

## 10. Multi-clinic installation

One installation can host several clinics (`facilities`).

- **Isolation.** Every patient, clinical, financial and ABDM table carries `facility_id` and is protected by PostgreSQL row-level security. The API sets the clinic for each database connection from the signed-in user. A query without a clinic context returns no rows, even if the application code is wrong. Users, roles and the audit log are scoped explicitly in code.
- **Administration.** The bootstrap administrator also holds `PLATFORM_ADMIN`, which can create clinics and change the shared system roles. Each clinic manages its own users, custom roles, services and identity details.

## 11. What still needs real data, integrations or validation

- **Clinical review of all starter content and citations** by qualified dermatologists. Without it, Panel A shows only *No approved clinical content*.
- **Diagnostic performance.** The rule weights are hand-set and have not been validated against labelled cases.
- **A licensed drug database** (CDSCO-approved product information, a comprehensive interaction source, pricing). The starter catalogue holds 53 medicines and 10 interaction pairs.
- **Real prescribing history.** Panels B and C show meaningful patterns only after enough real finalized cases. The demo seed creates synthetic cases in a single month, with invented prescribing habits.
- **Legal and governance basis** for network use under the DPDP Act, 2023 and its rules: data-sharing agreements, patient notices, a retention policy, and a formal re-identification risk assessment. ClinicFlow provides the technical controls, not the legal basis.
- **ABDM / FHIR mapping** of dermatology findings and images (Observation, Media, DocumentReference), and live ABDM integration (still mock).
- **Regulatory assessment** of the CDS as software that may be a medical device, before any clinical use.
